Glossary

The canonical vocabulary for this workspace. Where the literature uses several words for one idea, one is chosen here and the rest listed as aliases to avoid.

Prints to three pages · Keep open while reading papers

Where compute goes

Pre-training scaling
Improving a model by adding parameters, data and compute before any task exists. Predictable, expensive, and flattening since around 2024.Avoid: scaling laws, unqualified — there are now several
Train-time scaling
Post-training compute, mostly reinforcement learning against checkable rewards. Where reasoning models come from. You consume this axis; you rarely control it.Avoid: fine-tuning, which is a narrower thing
Test-time scaling
Compute spent during the request: longer thinking, repeated sampling, search, verification. Entirely a property of your harness.Avoid: inference optimisation — that usually means making inference cheaper, the opposite concern
Self-improvement
The loop where test-time compute manufactures verified successes that become supervision. Industrially: synthetic training data. Practically: accumulated verified experience.
Parallel sampling
Drawing k independent candidates from one prompt. Explores different approaches; parallelises, so k samples cost roughly one sample's latency.Avoid: batching, which is an infrastructure term
Sequential revision
One draft, improved over several turns. Refines a single approach; serial in latency; near-worthless without real feedback between turns.

Measuring

Coverage / pass@k
Fraction of problems solved by at least one of k samples. What your system could achieve with a perfect selector. An upper bound, not a result.Avoid: accuracy at k
pass@1
Fraction solved on a single attempt. What you ship, unless you have built a selector.
pass^k
Fraction of problems solved on all k independent attempts. A reliability metric: it punishes variance where pass@k rewards it. Almost always the honest number for a production workflow.Avoid: confusing with pass@k — the notation is cruel and the meanings are opposite
Generation–verification gap
The distance between coverage and what your selector actually delivers. Usually the dominant term in agent underperformance.Avoid: generator–verifier gap (same thing, less common)
Majority voting / self-consistency
Selecting the modal answer across samples. Suppresses idiosyncratic noise; structurally blind to rare-correct answers, so it plateaus after roughly 10–50 samples.
Best-of-n
Generate n candidates, score each with a verifier or reward model, return the highest. The standard baseline.

Verification

Verifier
Anything that scores an output's correctness. Ranges from a compiler (near-perfect) to a prompted judge (weak). The umbrella term.
Outcome reward model (ORM)
Scores the final answer only. Cheap labels; blind to right-answers-by-wrong-reasoning; overfits its training distribution more than you would like.
Process reward model (PRM)
Scores each intermediate step. Catches false positives, finds rare-correct solutions, generalises further, and can prune search mid-generation. Costs step-level labels and adds a threshold to tune.
Rollout labelling
Scoring a step by how often completions from that point reach a correct final answer. Hard estimate: any success counts. Soft estimate: the success fraction. Removes the need for human step annotation.Avoid: Monte Carlo supervision, unless you mean it precisely
Weak verifier
A verifier correlated with correctness but reliably imperfect — most LLM judges, most reward models. The realistic case, and the assumption the Weaver line of work is built on.
Verifier ensemble
Several weak verifiers, filtered for quality then combined with learned weights. Requires that their errors be independent, which prompts of the same model are not.
LLM-as-judge
Using a model to score outputs. Known biases: position, verbosity, self-preference, style-over-substance, and poor calibration on numeric scales.Avoid: AI evaluator — too vague to design against
Public / private check split
Showing an agent some checks to iterate against while scoring on held-out ones. The defence against your agent optimising your assertions instead of the task.

Architecture

Fusion
Handing a model several candidate answers and asking it to synthesise one. Can exceed oracle selection, because it composes partial merits rather than choosing among them.Avoid: aggregation, which suggests voting
Critic
An operation producing a written critique of a candidate, consumed by a later ranker or fuser. Specific instructions ("list unsupported claims") beat general ones ("critique this").
Ranker
Prompted ordering of candidates by quality. Cheap; better than random; well short of an oracle.
ReAct loop
Interleaved thought → action → observation, with each thought appended to context. The base pattern of nearly every tool-using agent.
Orchestrator / planner–worker
A central model decomposes a task and dispatches sub-tasks. Distinct from chaining, where the decomposition is fixed in advance by you.
Error cascading
One wrong observation or step contaminating everything downstream, with the agent rarely revisiting it. The dominant failure of long-horizon runs.
Constitution
An explicit written set of principles a good output must satisfy. Doubles as a critique prompt and an eval rubric. The fallback when no oracle exists.

Things people confuse

  • Coverage is not accuracy. pass@k is what the model could do; pass@1 is what you ship. Reporting the first as if it were the second is the most common overstatement in agent demos.
  • pass@k and pass^k are opposites. One asks "ever succeeds", the other "always succeeds". For anything running unattended, you want the second.
  • A verifier is not an evaluator. A verifier runs inside the loop to select outputs; an evaluator runs outside it to measure the system. Use the same component for both and your eval is measuring itself.
  • More samples is not always better. Past some k, a weak verifier starts selecting confident wrong answers. Sweep k and find where your curve turns over.
  • An ensemble of one model is not an ensemble. Independence of errors is what buys signal, and three prompts of one model share every blind spot.
  • Test-time compute does not manufacture missing capability. On the hardest tail, a stronger model still wins at any realistic budget.
