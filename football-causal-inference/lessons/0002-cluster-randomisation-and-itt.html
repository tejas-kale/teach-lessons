Respect the unit that was randomised

Your win: identify the correct experimental unit and interpret an intention-to-treat effect.

Paper. Al Attar et al. randomised 21 amateur men’s teams: 10 teams (160 players) received pre- and post-training FIFA 11+, while 11 teams (184 players) received the pre-training programme alone.

Why teams, not players?

Players train together, so player-level allocation risks contamination. Team randomisation protects delivery but creates correlated outcomes within teams. The effective independent information is closer to 21 clusters than 344 unrelated players.

ITT = E[Y | assigned extra post-training] − E[Y | assigned control]

Worked reasoning

The experimental group reported 26 injuries and the control group 82 over one season. The paper analysed generalised estimating equations using intention to treat. The ITT estimand is the effect of assignment to the programme policy, including imperfect adherence — not the biological effect among only perfect compliers.

Checks that preserve the design

  • Report allocation concealment and baseline balance, but do not test many outcomes until one “wins”.
  • Use cluster-aware uncertainty; individual-level standard errors would be too optimistic.
  • Keep teams in assigned groups for ITT, even when delivery is imperfect.
  • Define exposure time and injury surveillance consistently across arms.
