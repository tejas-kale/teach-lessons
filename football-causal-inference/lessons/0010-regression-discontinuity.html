Threshold designs · 15 minutes

Estimate the effect at the cut-off

Your win: interpret a sharp RDD as a local effect rather than a league-wide average.

Papers. Reilly & Witt use the Scottish Premiership sixth/seventh-place split to estimate attendance effects. Speer studies teams just across promotion and relegation cut-offs.

τ_RD = limₓ↓c E[Y|X=x] − limₓ↑c E[Y|X=x]

Worked reasoning

Compare clubs narrowly above and below the split threshold, fitting separate local trends in the running variable. The discontinuity estimates the effect for clubs at the cut-off. It does not directly tell us the effect for the league winner or bottom club.

Credibility checks

  • Continuity: absent treatment, expected attendance or later performance changes smoothly through the threshold.
  • No precise manipulation: teams cannot sort around the cut-off in a way tied to potential outcomes.
  • Local fit: show a binned plot, use a defensible bandwidth and avoid high-order global polynomials.
  • Placebos: no jumps in predetermined covariates or at fake cut-offs.

Promotion/relegation can be fuzzy if the threshold changes the probability rather than deterministically assigning treatment; then the discontinuity in treatment is an instrument for a local complier effect.

Who does a sharp RDD primarily describe?

Primary reading: Reilly & Witt (2021); Speer (2023); Angrist & Pischke, Chapter 4. Ask your teaching agent to distinguish sharp from fuzzy assignment.
