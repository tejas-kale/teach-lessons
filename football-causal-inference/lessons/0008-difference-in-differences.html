Natural experiments · 13 minutes

Subtract the change that would have happened anyway

Your win: construct a DiD contrast and state the parallel-trends assumption in football language.

Paper. Scoppa used COVID-19 closed-door matches across five countries and ten seasons to study how crowd absence changed player and referee behaviour.

DiD = (Ȳ treated,post − Ȳ treated,pre) − (Ȳ control,post − Ȳ control,pre)

Toy calculation

Home teams’ goal difference falls from +0.40 to +0.10 in closed-door matches: −0.30. In the comparison group it falls from +0.35 to +0.25: −0.10. The DiD estimate is −0.20 goals. It is causal only if the treated group would otherwise have followed the comparison group’s change.

Parallel trends is counterfactual

Similar pre-trends support — but cannot prove — the assumption. Pandemic timing also changed congestion, player fitness, travel and rules. A defensible design explains why those co-interventions do not differentially alter the treated trajectory.

  • No anticipation before stadium closure.
  • No treatment-induced changes to group composition that redefine outcomes.
  • Stable measurement and scheduling.
  • Appropriate clustering for the assignment and serial correlation structure.
