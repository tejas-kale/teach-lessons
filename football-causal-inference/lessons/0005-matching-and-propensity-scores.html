Selection on observables · 14 minutes

Build a credible match-day comparison

Your win: use a propensity score to compare unusual match days with comparable usual days.

Paper. Goller & Krumer studied 10,142 matches in England, France, Germany and Spain, using radius matching on the propensity score to compare non-frequent with frequent match days.

e(X) = P(A=1 | X)    and under exchangeability   Y(a) ⟂ A | X

A design, not a prediction contest

  1. Choose only pre-kick-off common causes of scheduling and outcomes.
  2. Estimate each match’s probability of an unusual day.
  3. Check overlap; discard treated matches without credible controls.
  4. Match within a radius, then inspect covariate balance.
  5. Estimate the treatment effect in the matched sample and state its target population.

Worked reasoning

A Sunday-night derby should not be matched to a routine Saturday afternoon match merely because their propensity scores are numerically close if key covariates remain imbalanced. The propensity score compresses measured covariates; it does not repair missing confounders or guarantee balance.

  • Conditional exchangeability given measured scheduling, team and match variables.
  • Common support across day types.
  • Consistent definitions of attendance and home advantage.
  • No conditioning on consequences of the scheduled day, such as actual crowd size when crowd size is part of the pathway.
