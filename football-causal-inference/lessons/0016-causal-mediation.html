Ask how an intervention works

Your win: distinguish total, direct and indirect effects — and see why randomising treatment is not enough.

Paper. Røynesdal et al. studied mediation in the EuroFIT randomised programme, delivered through 15 professional football clubs to 1,113 male supporters. The units are supporters, not players or matches, and mediated effects were not robust.

Natural direct effect
Effect not transmitted through that mediator.

Why treatment randomisation is insufficient

Randomisation protects the programme–outcome comparison. The mediator — autonomous motivation — is not randomised. Unmeasured causes of motivation and later behaviour can confound the mediator–outcome relation. Treatment-induced mediator–outcome confounders create further difficulty.

  • No unmeasured treatment–outcome, treatment–mediator or mediator–outcome confounding.
  • No mediator–outcome confounder affected by treatment for natural effects.
  • Well-defined, temporally ordered mediator and outcome.
  • Sensitivity analysis treats mediation conclusions as more assumption-dependent than the total ITT effect.
