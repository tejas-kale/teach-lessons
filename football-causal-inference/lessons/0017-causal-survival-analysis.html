Time-to-event outcomes · 15 minutes

Estimate injury risk over football exposure

Your win: choose an interpretable survival estimand and handle censoring as a causal problem.

Paper. Rössler et al. randomised clubs to “11+ Kids” and observed injuries over exposure time, analysing time to injury with mixed-effects Cox models.
Risk by week 20
P(T≤20 under programme) versus control; directly answers “how many are injured?”
Restricted mean injury-free survival time
Expected injury-free weeks up to a fixed horizon; an absolute time scale.
Hazard ratio
Instantaneous rate contrast among those still event-free; conditional risk sets change over time.

Censoring

A player transferred at week 12 has unknown later injury time. Treating this as harmless requires independent censoring, possibly conditional on measured history. Inverse-probability-of-censoring weights can reconstruct the target risk set if the censoring model, exchangeability and positivity are adequate.

Randomisation identifies assignment effects, but non-adherence, informative dropout, competing events and exposure-time definitions still shape interpretation. Proportional hazards is a modelling assumption; a hazard ratio is not a risk ratio.
