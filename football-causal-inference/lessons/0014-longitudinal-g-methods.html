Time-varying treatment · 17 minutes

Handle treatment-confounder feedback

Your win: recognise when ordinary time-varying regression cannot estimate a training strategy effect.

Evidence-gap lesson. No credible football application of longitudinal g-methods was located. This is a constructed season-long training-load scenario, grounded in the injury studies but not attributed to them.
Scenario. Each week, coaches choose high or moderate training load. Current fatigue predicts next week’s load and injury, but is itself changed by earlier load.

Ignoring fatigue confounds later treatment. Adjusting conventionally for current fatigue can block part of earlier load’s effect and induce bias. This treatment-confounder feedback is exactly why longitudinal g-methods exist.

Parametric g-formula
Model the longitudinal data process, simulate every player under each load strategy, then average injuries.
Marginal structural model
Weight each observed history by inverse probabilities of its treatment sequence to create sequential balance.
Structural nested model
Use g-estimation to find the effect that removes association between treatment and “blipped-down” outcomes.

Identification

At every week: consistency, sequential exchangeability given measured history, and positivity for each strategy. Near-deterministic load rules create extreme product weights; truncation trades variance for bias and must be reported.

What creates treatment-confounder feedback here?

Primary reading: Hernán & Robins, What If, Chapters 19–21. Ask your teaching agent to compare the three g-methods on one two-week example.
