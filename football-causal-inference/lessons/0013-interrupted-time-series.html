Time series · 13 minutes

Separate a level jump from a slope change

Your win: specify a segmented model and explain its fragile counterfactual.

Paper. Duque-Arias et al. followed muscular injuries among 94 Colombian professional players before and during the pandemic return-to-competition interruption.

Yₜ = β₀ + β₁·timeₜ + β₂·postₜ + β₃·time_afterₜ + εₜ

β₂: level change
Immediate jump at the interruption.
β₃: slope change
Change in trajectory after the interruption.

Worked reasoning

The counterfactual extends the pre-interruption injury trend beyond the return date. That is credible only if no other co-occurring change — training load, fixture congestion, surveillance, squad rotation or infection — would have bent the trend.

Checks

  • Enough pre- and post-period observations to estimate trends.
  • Seasonality and changing exposure hours are modelled.
  • Residual autocorrelation is diagnosed and reflected in uncertainty.
  • Intervention timing is not chosen after seeing the outcome series.
  • A comparison series or multiple interruptions can strengthen attribution.
Why inspect residual autocorrelation?
