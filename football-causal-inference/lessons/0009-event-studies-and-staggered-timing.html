Your win: read leads and lags without treating a conventional two-way fixed-effects plot as automatically causal.

Paper. Breidenbach & Mitze compared German districts hosting professional football matches with comparable districts to study COVID-19 infections, using dynamic panel estimates and synthetic controls.

Read the graph conceptually

Lead coefficients (k<0) probe whether outcomes were already diverging before hosting. Lag coefficients (k≥0) describe how effects evolve after exposure. Omit one pre-period, commonly k=−1, as the reference.

The staggered-adoption trap

When districts are treated at different times and effects vary, conventional two-way fixed effects can compare already-treated units with newly treated ones and attach negative weights. Prefer cohort-by-time effects or another estimator designed for staggered timing, then aggregate transparently.

  • No differential pre-trends or anticipation.
  • Comparison districts remain valid at each cohort’s treatment time.
  • Spillovers across district borders are limited or modelled.
  • Testing many leads and lags calls for joint inference, not selective storytelling.
