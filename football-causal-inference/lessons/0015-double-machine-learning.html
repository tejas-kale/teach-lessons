Flexible nuisance models · 16 minutes

Cross-fit before estimating formation effects

Your win: explain why DML residualises treatment and outcome on separate folds.

Preprint. Ruiz-Menárguez & Badiella analyse more than 22,000 fixtures using cross-fitted XGBoost nuisance models to estimate effects of categorical formations. It is a new, non-peer-reviewed preprint and should not be the sole authority on DML.

Partialling out

  1. Split matches into folds, respecting leakage risks such as seasons or teams.
  2. On other folds, predict outcome m(X) and formation assignment e(X).
  3. For the held-out fold, form outcome and treatment residuals.
  4. Estimate the effect from residual-on-residual variation.
  5. Rotate folds and combine estimates with valid uncertainty.

Y − m̂(X) = θ[A − ê(X)] + residual

Bad controls remain bad

Post-kick-off possession, shots and expected goals may mediate formation effects. Powerful learners do not make them safe controls. Cross-fitting reduces overfit bias in nuisance estimation; it cannot repair the wrong adjustment set or unmeasured tactical confounding.

For categorical formations, specify contrasts — for example 4-3-3 versus 4-4-2 — and ensure overlap. A vague “formation effect” hides multiple treatments and target populations.
