Turn a football hunch into a causal estimand

Your win: rewrite “Do new coaches help?” as a comparison of well-defined counterfactual outcomes.

Question. For clubs that dismiss a coach after matchweek 10, what would their next-five-match points be if they dismissed now versus if they retained the coach?

The missing match

For club i, let Yᵢ(1) be next-five-match points under dismissal and Yᵢ(0) under retention. We observe only one. The causal effect for that club, Yᵢ(1) − Yᵢ(0), therefore cannot be read directly from results.

ATE = E[Y(1) − Y(0)]    ATT = E[Y(1) − Y(0) | A=1]

Population
Eligible clubs at matchweek 10.
Treatment
Dismiss within the next 48 hours.
Outcome
Points in the next five league matches.
Contrast
Dismissal versus retention.

Worked reasoning

A dismissed club earns seven points. That is an observed outcome, not an effect. Comparing it with the club’s previous five matches mixes treatment with time. Comparing it with clubs that kept their coaches mixes treatment with selection. A credible design must identify the missing Y(0) for dismissed clubs, if the target is ATT.

Identification before estimation

  • Consistency: “dismissal” is specified well enough that observed points equal the relevant potential outcome.
  • Exchangeability: the comparison can stand in for the missing counterfactual, possibly after adjustment.
  • Positivity: each relevant club profile has some chance of both strategies.
  • No interference: one club’s dismissal does not change another club’s outcome — questionable in a shared league.
Which is the best-defined causal question?

Primary reading: Hernán & Robins, What If, Chapters 1 and 3. Football anchor: De Paola & Scoppa (2012). Ask your teaching agent about any unclear distinction between an outcome and an effect.
