Natural experiments · 16 minutes

Use policy shifts to isolate treatment variation

Your win: defend — or reject — an instrument using four explicit conditions.

Paper. Glennon et al. use changes in national restrictions on foreign players as an instrument for immigrant starters, estimating effects on goal difference and win probability.
Relevance
Restrictions change immigrant participation.
Independence
Policy variation is as-if unrelated to clubs’ potential performance.
Exclusion
Restrictions affect outcomes only through immigrant starters.
Monotonicity
Easing restrictions does not make some clubs use fewer immigrant starters for the opposite causal reason.

Wald LATE = [E(Y|Z=1)−E(Y|Z=0)] / [E(A|Z=1)−E(A|Z=0)]

2SLS interpretation

Stage 1 predicts immigrant starters from the policy instrument and controls. Stage 2 relates performance to predicted immigrant starters. With heterogeneous effects and monotonicity, the result is a local average treatment effect for clubs whose immigrant use responds to the policy — not necessarily every club.

A strong first-stage statistic supports relevance only. It cannot test exclusion. Direct policy effects on transfer prices, squad registration or domestic-player development threaten exclusion.

Which assumption is not established by a strong first stage?
