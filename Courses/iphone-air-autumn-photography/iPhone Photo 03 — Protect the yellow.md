Overcast Berlin is a gift. Clipped foliage is not. Meter the leaf, not the trunk.

≈8 minutes · Week 1 walk

Autumn in Berlin is often overcast. Clouds are a giant softbox: yellows and reds record without July’s specular glare. What fails on this phone is the **bright leaf against a darker trunk**. Smart HDR 5 tries to hold both, and the leaf goes creamy.

You can lift shadows five to fifteen points in Photos. You cannot restore a clipped yellow. That asymmetry is the whole lesson.

## Practice on the corner, not in theory

Apple’s shot setup is the whole toolbox: tap to focus and expose, drag the sun to bias exposure, touch-and-hold for *AE/AF Lock*.[¹](#s1)

1.  Photo mode, 1×, Standard style. Live Photos off if you want the option of 48MP later; for this week, 24MP is enough.
2.  Tap the **brightest leaves** you care about. Drag the exposure sun **down** until those leaves have texture, even if the shadows go a bit dark.
3.  For mixed sun and shade, hold until AE/AF Lock appears, recompose, then nudge exposure.
4.  Backlight: stand so the sun is behind the canopy. Expose for the glow in the leaves, not the sky. A pale sky is acceptable. A silhouette of trunks against colour is a valid picture.
5.  Golden hour in October is short and low. Side light rakes bark and cobbles. Front light (sun behind you) is the boring default — walk until the light is beside or behind the subject.
6.  Blue hour and wet streets: let Night mode appear. Hold still; the shutter is long. Rest the Air against a pole. Do not expect 48MP.

Weather to chase: high cloud, fog, rain just after it stops (puddles), a hard freeze if it comes. Do not wait for a perfect sunset. If the week is rainy, that week’s lesson is weather, not a skip.

## Check yourself

Leaves look like creamy yellow blobs. What did you likely do?

- [ ] Metered the trunk or sky, clipping the leaf.
- [ ] Metered the leaf, then lifted shadows slightly.
- [ ] Locked AE/AF on the leaf, then recomposed.
- [ ] Waited for high cloud instead of harsh sun.

> [!tip]- Reveal the answer
> B–D are the intended technique. Creamy yellow is almost always highlight clip from metering the dark thing or the sky. Fix: tap the leaf, expose down.

> [!abstract] Assignment 1 · one corner, three visits
> Pick one street corner within ten minutes of home. Photograph it morning, overcast midday, and the last 30 minutes before sunset. Same standpoint, same 1× framing, plus one extra frame each visit at 2×. Keep six photos. Edit cap: two minutes per image — straighten; crop empty bright sky rather than crushing it; Highlights −10 to −25 if leaves are hot; Shadows +5 to +15 on the dusk set; warmth only if the file looks colder than the scene you remember. Stop. Publish this week: three images (one per visit) and a paragraph on which light made the colour believable. Photos → ⓘ if you want the time of day in the caption.

## Retrieval Practice

In two sentences: why is overcast an advantage here, and what must you still do with the exposure sun?

> [!example]- Model answer
> Clouds even out the light so foliage is not full of specular glare. You still tap the brightest leaves you care about and drag exposure down, because HDR will happily cream those leaves to hold the trunk.

**Bring a file, not a vibe.** If a yellow still looks wrong after the two-minute edit, ask with the unedited HEIF in front of you. Guessing sliders in the abstract will not help.
