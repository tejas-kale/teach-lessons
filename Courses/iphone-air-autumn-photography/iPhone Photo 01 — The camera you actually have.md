Before any walk: one rear sensor, four honest framings, and a hard ban on Pro-camera folklore.

≈8 minutes · Unlocks every later assignment

The month of photographs in [issue \#12](https://github.com/tejas-kale/blog/issues/12) is not “iPhone photography.” It is photography on an *iPhone Air*. Apple’s spec sheet is blunt: one 48MP Fusion Main at 26 mm, ƒ/1.6, sensor-shift OIS; a 12MP optical-quality 2× at 52 mm from that same sensor; HEIF and JPEG only.[¹](#s1) There is no ultra-wide, no dedicated telephoto, no macro mode, no ProRAW, no Apple Log.

If a tip needs any of those, it is someone else’s phone. Discard it.

![[5659e8504102ba573ce6c9ee52c12f0fba251f06.svg]]

*Think in four framings, all from the Fusion Main. Panorama (up to 63MP on this phone) is the substitute for an ultra-wide, not a fifth lens.*

## What “2×” is, and is not

Apple lists “12MP optical-quality 2x Telephoto: 52 mm, ƒ/1.6 … sensor-shift optical image stabilization.”[¹](#s1) The word *Telephoto* on that line is marketing for a crop. There is no second glass assembly. You get a tighter field of view and a 12MP file, still stabilised. That is enough for a tree against a façade, or a leaf you cannot step to. It is not binoculars.

Digital zoom is listed up to 10×. For stills you intend to publish, treat anything past 2× as a viewfinder, then walk.

**Panorama** (up to 63MP) is how this phone gets wide. Rotate from the hips with locked elbows. Use it for a canal, an avenue, or a courtyard you cannot step back from. Do not panorama a scene with moving trams unless you want stitches.

## Check yourself

The Air’s 2× “telephoto” is which of these?

- [ ] A second lens with its own 52 mm glass.
- [ ] A twelve-megapixel crop of the Fusion sensor.
- [ ] A ten-times digital zoom with extra sharpening.
- [ ] A sixty-three-megapixel panorama swept from the hips.

> [!tip]- Reveal the answer
> Apple’s spec sheet: 12MP optical-quality 2× at 52 mm from the Fusion Main, same ƒ/1.6 and sensor-shift OIS. No second lens. 10× is digital zoom. 63MP is panorama — the ultra-wide substitute, not 2×.

Each option is ten words. If you picked D, you remembered a real Air feature and attached it to the wrong name.

## Why this is the first lesson

The blog series will fail in a specific way if this map is fuzzy: you will stand at 26 mm, eye-height, and wait for an ultra-wide foreground that never arrives. Or you will pinch to 5× and publish mush. The assignment below is a control so later weeks are comparable — same tree, same standpoint, four framings.

> [!abstract] Assignment 0 · tonight
> Photograph one tree you will reuse all month. Four frames: 1×, 28 mm, 35 mm, 2×, same standpoint. No editing. Look at them on a larger screen. Notice how 26 mm includes parked cars and sky, and 52 mm turns the tree into a subject.

## Retrieval Practice

Name the Air’s rear camera in one sentence: sensor, equivalent focal length, and the one “telephoto” you actually have.

> [!example]- Model answer
> One 48MP Fusion Main, 26 mm ƒ/1.6, plus an optical-quality 2× crop at 52 mm from that same sensor. No second lens.

**This is a conversation, not a document.** If a setting on your Air does not match a name in this lesson (28/35 mm menu wording moves between iOS versions), ask. The idea is “crops of one sensor,” not a screenshot.
